Redesign & Design System

Community Ordering — from screenshots to a real product

A full redesign of the three supplied screens plus the states the flow was missing, built on one reusable design system. The core idea — neighbors combine orders to get wholesale pricing — is preserved everywhere; what changes is how clearly the app explains where an order stands, what's committed vs. estimated, and what happens next.

1 · What wasn't working

Trust signals were miscolored

The "never exceeds MRP" price‑protection guarantee — a reassurance — was styled as a red alert box. Red should mean something is wrong. Reassurance and risk were visually swapped.

No sense of where an order stood

Pooling, bidding, invoicing, payment and delivery are five distinct states, but nothing on any screen showed which one you were in, or what came next. Users could confirm a pledge and then have no way to check on it.

Metrics competed with each other

"148 Houses Active," "148 units pooled (42 houses)," and "210 units pooled (65 houses)" all appear on one screen with no shared meaning — active in the tower? ordered this cycle? ordered this item? Same number, different referents.

Savings were repeated until they felt like a pitch

The invoice stated savings four separate times (per line ×3, plus a total, plus "beat the benchmark"). One clear total is more credible than four reminders.

Tone drifted from the rest of the app

A 🎉 emoji banner on the invoice sits oddly next to GST breakdowns and reverse-auction mechanics elsewhere. This app is closer to a ledger than a deals feed — the copy should read that way throughout.

Flow stopped at payment

There was no order list, no post-payment tracking, no way to leave a pledge before it locks, and no loading/empty/error states — all needed for this to hold together as a real product.

2 · Design system

Color — semantic tokens

One brand color used only for primary actions and current-state emphasis. Success/amber/danger are reserved strictly for what they mean — amber for time pressure, danger only for things that actually failed.

Typography

IBM Plex Sans for interface text; IBM Plex Mono, tabular, for every number that represents money, quantity, a countdown or an ID. Splitting the two makes the app read like a ledger — appropriate for a product built on pooled billing and invoices — and keeps prices scannable and unambiguous.

4 · Flow changes & missing states

01 — Mid‑cycle order tracking

Added. Previously a household confirmed its pledge and had nowhere to check on it until the invoice appeared. Now it's one tap away from the Orders tab at every stage.

02 — Leave an order before bidding closes

Added. The cart's commitment text now says explicitly that leaving is possible, and the tracking screen carries a low‑emphasis "Leave this order" action while pooling/bidding is still open. It disappears once bidding closes, since the pool is locked at that point.

03 — Post‑payment delivery tracking

Added. The original flow ended at "Pay now." Households pooling grocery money need to know it arrived and where to collect it — this closes that gap.

04 — Orders list with active/past split

Added. Referenced by the bottom nav in the originals but never designed. Doubles as the entry point for tracking and for reordering from a past cycle.

05 — One participation metric, not three

Changed. The hub now leads with a single "households joined this cycle" figure; per‑product counts stay as secondary, clearly‑scoped social proof rather than competing headline numbers.

06 — Loading, empty and error states

Added throughout: skeleton rows for the product list, an empty cart, an empty order history, a failed hub load, and a failed payment — none of which existed in the source screens.
